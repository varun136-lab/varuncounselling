My philosophy

I'm not trying to be in your life forever. I'm trying to make myself unnecessary.

We work on the problem together, and along the way you build the tools to handle it yourself. Most people reach a point where the weekly hour is no longer what they need, and that's the goal. And of course, the door stays open for a tune-up whenever you need it.

How the work splits

First, always I hear your story I want to hear it in your words what has been happening, how long it has been going on, and what you have already tried. The direction we take comes out of that, not out of a template.
Route one Somatic Starting with the body. Breath, tension, and what your nervous system does before your thoughts catch up.
Route two BehaviouralStarting with the patterns. The thoughts, habits, and small daily choices that keep the pattern running.
Both routes are tied together with mindfulness. Whichever way we go, the through-line is noticing what is happening as it happens. That is what makes the change stick once sessions end.

About

Hi, I'm Varun.

I'm drawn to what lies beneath the surface. The patterns, stories and experiences that shape how we see ourselves and move through the world. My own journey through strict meditation taught me that meaningful change happens in the relationship we have with ourselves.

I bring a calm, curious, grounded presence to therapy. It is non-judgmental, and genuinely interested in who you are, not just what you're struggling with. I also believe warmth and rigour aren't opposites, and I hold both.

My approach is conversational rather than clinical. I ask questions, I keep track of the threads, and I'll gently point out patterns when I notice them. What we talk about stays between us.

Who I work with

Individuals (18+) and teens (16+) — and anyone wanting a kinder relationship with themselves.

My approach

I work integratively, drawing on a range of evidence-based approaches — Compassion-Focused Therapy, CBT, somatic and mindfulness-based methods, IFS and Emotionally Focused Therapy — tailored to what you actually need, not a fixed formula.

Sessions have a rhythm: we ground, we explore, we work, and we close with grounding again. I tend to work at the roots rather than only the symptoms, and I'm particularly drawn to the relationship between shame, self-criticism, and all the ways we've learned to protect ourselves from the world.

I also believe psychoeducation is a powerful tool — understanding why you feel or respond the way you do can be its own form of relief. And I love a good metaphor.
