My philosophy

I'm not trying to be in your life forever. I'm trying to make myself unnecessary.

We work on the problem together, and along the way you build the tools to handle it yourself. Most people reach a point where the weekly hour is no longer what they need, and that's the goal. And of course, the door stays open for a tune-up whenever you need it.

About

Hi, I'm Varun.

My own journey through the performing arts, facilatation and strict meditation taught me that meaningful change happens in the relationship we have with ourselves.

I bring a calm, curious, and grounded presence to therapy. My approach is non-judgmental and genuinely interested in who you are as a whole person, beyond the problem that brought you in. I believe warmth and rigour belong together, and I try to offer both. 

Our conversations are natural and collaborative. I ask thoughtful questions, keep track of the threads that matter, and gently reflect patterns that may be difficult to see from the inside. You can expect a space that feels private, respectful, and honest.

My approach

I work integratively, drawing on a range of evidence-based approaches — EMDR, CBT, somatic and mindfulness-based methods, IFS and Emotionally Focused Therapy — tailored to what you actually need, not a fixed formula.

Sessions have a rhythm: we ground, we explore, we work, and we close with grounding again. I tend to work at the roots rather than only the symptoms, and I'm particularly drawn to the relationship between shame, self-criticism, and all the ways we've learned to protect ourselves from the world.

I also believe psychoeducation is a powerful tool — understanding why you feel or respond the way you do can be its own form of relief. And I love a good metaphor.

Questions people ask

How do I know if my anxiety is severe enough for therapy?

If worry, stress, or panic is affecting your sleep, relationships, work, or your ability to enjoy life, therapy can help. You do not need a diagnosis to start.

What is the best treatment for panic attacks?

CBT combined with exposure work and nervous-system regulation has the strongest research support. We tailor it to your comfort level.

Does avoidance really make anxiety worse?

Yes. Avoidance reduces fear in the moment but strengthens anxiety over time. Therapy helps you break that cycle safely and gradually.

Do you help with driving anxiety or fear after an accident?

Yes. That includes trauma-informed anxiety therapy and return-to-driving readiness work.

Can therapy help if medication is not working?

Yes. Therapy can complement medication or stand on its own, and many people benefit from both together.

Do you offer online sessions?

Yes — secure video sessions, or in person in Toronto by availability.

Will therapy give me tools I can use immediately?

Most people start using practical anxiety-regulation tools within the first one or two sessions.

I feel ashamed about my anxiety. Is therapy judgment-free?

Completely. Anxiety is extremely common and highly treatable, and nothing you bring here will be met with judgment.

Questions people ask

Is depression treatable?

Yes. Evidence-based therapies show strong outcomes across many forms of depression, including chronic, trauma-related, seasonal, and postpartum.

Do I need a diagnosis to start therapy?

No. Many people begin counselling before any formal diagnosis, and support is available at any stage.

Is therapy or medication better for depression?

Both can work. Some people do well with therapy alone, others combine the two, and I am happy to work alongside your physician or psychiatrist.

How long does depression therapy take?

It depends on intensity and circumstances. Many people notice improvement within six to ten sessions, while deeper work takes longer.

Does this help with burnout or chronic stress?

Yes. Burnout and depression overlap heavily, and therapy helps clarify what you are actually dealing with and build a recovery plan.

What if I do not have the energy for therapy?

That is expected, and we plan around it. Early sessions ask very little of you between meetings.

EMDR Therapy for Trauma, PTSD & Limiting Beliefs

A powerful, evidence-based therapy that helps the brain heal from trauma, distressing memories, and overwhelming emotional experiences.

EMDR is a structured approach designed to process painful memories, reduce emotional distress, and help you move forward with clarity. Unlike traditional talk therapy, it uses bilateral stimulation — eye movements, tapping, or sound — to support your brain's natural healing process, letting stuck or traumatic experiences be reprocessed safely. It is widely used for PTSD, complex trauma, anxiety, panic, chronic stress, childhood trauma, phobias, grief, and emotional triggers, and it is often the preferred option for people who find talking about trauma difficult or who feel stuck after other approaches.

The tools

1. History & treatment planning We explore your goals, your triggers, and the memories or experiences that feel stuck.
2. Preparation & stabilization We build coping tools, grounding strategies, and emotional safety before any processing begins.
3. Assessment We identify the target memory along with the body sensations, emotions, and negative beliefs attached to it.
4. Desensitization Eye movements, tapping, or auditory tones help the brain process the memory without overwhelm.
5. Installation of positive beliefs We strengthen new, healthier beliefs to replace the old trauma-driven ones.
6. Body scan We check how the memory sits in your body and process anything still lingering there.
7. Closure You leave every session grounded, safe, and regulated. Nothing ends mid-wave.
8. Re-evaluation At the start of each new session we review progress and decide the next step together.

Questions people ask

Does EMDR really work?

Yes. It is strongly supported by research and recommended for trauma treatment by bodies including the World Health Organization.

Do I need to talk about my trauma in detail?

No. EMDR does not require detailed retelling, which makes it well suited to people who find storytelling overwhelming or retraumatizing.

How many sessions will I need?

It varies. A single event often processes in six to ten sessions; complex trauma usually needs longer-term work.

Is EMDR safe online?

Yes. It can be delivered effectively over secure video using visual or auditory bilateral stimulation.

Will I feel overwhelmed or activated?

I pace the work carefully and we prepare grounding tools first, so you stay regulated and safe throughout.

Can EMDR help with anxiety or panic attacks?

Yes. It can target both the past events that created the anxiety and the beliefs that keep it running.

Behavioural Addiction Counselling

When the scrolling, the gaming, or the porn has stopped being a choice and started running the day.

Most people who come here have already tried to cut down on their own, more than once. The problem is rarely willpower. Screens, games, and porn are very good at doing a job — numbing boredom, dropping stress, filling loneliness, avoiding something harder — and until that job gets done another way, the habit keeps winning. We work out what the behaviour is actually for, what sets it off, and what a realistic relationship with it looks like for you. No shame, no lectures, and no assumption that quitting forever is the only answer.

Questions people ask

Do I have to quit completely?

Not unless you want to. For plenty of people a workable limit is the goal, and we test what holds.

Is this a real addiction?

The label matters less than the pattern. If it is compulsive, escalating, and costing you things, it is worth working on.

Will you judge me about porn?

No. It is a common reason people come in, and I talk about it the same way I talk about anything else.

Can my partner be involved?

Sometimes, and it can help. We would talk about what to share and when before anyone else joins.

Do blockers and app limits work?

They help as scaffolding, but on their own they get worked around. They do more once the underlying reason is being addressed.

How long does it take?

Most people notice a shift in the first six to eight sessions, with longer work if there is trauma or depression underneath.

Questions people ask

What is ambiguous loss?

It is loss without closure — someone physically present but psychologically absent, as with dementia or addiction, or physically absent but emotionally present, as with estrangement or a missing person. The defining feature is chronic uncertainty.

How is grief counselling different from regular therapy?

It is built specifically for the emotional, cognitive, spiritual, and relational effects of loss, using frameworks for meaning-making, memory integration, and coping.

Do I need to have lost someone to come?

No. Loss also includes divorce, infertility, immigration, identity transitions, and chronic illness.

Is online grief counselling effective?

Yes. Research shows virtual grief therapy works as well as in person, and it lets you do this from home.

Can I bring faith, spirituality, or cultural tradition into this?

Absolutely. I work respectfully with religious and non-religious healing traditions alike.

It has been years. Is it too late?

No. Grief does not expire, and delayed grief is extremely common.

Emotional Regulation + Expression Counselling

For feeling too much, feeling too little, or never quite knowing how to say it out loud.

Some people go from zero to a hundred and cannot find the brakes. Others go quiet, flat, or numb and cannot find the accelerator. Both are regulation problems, and both are workable. We look at what your emotions are actually doing, where the pattern was learned, and how to build a wider range — so you can feel something strongly without it running the day, and say what you mean without rehearsing it for a week first.

The tools

DBT emotion-regulation skills Structured skills training in identifying emotions, tracking intensity, and applying a graded response — so escalation is interrupted early rather than managed after the fact.
Distress tolerance Skills for tolerating acute distress without acting on it, drawn from DBT: paced breathing, temperature change, grounding, and self-soothing through the peak.
Emotion-Focused Therapy (EFT) Working with primary emotion beneath the presenting one. Anger frequently masks hurt; flatness frequently masks fear. EFT accesses and processes the underlying emotion directly.
Somatic and nervous-system work Emotion registers physiologically before it registers cognitively. We build interoceptive awareness — breath, muscular tension, heart rate — so escalation is detected at onset.
Compassion-focused therapy Targets secondary shame — the self-critical response that follows emotional expression, and which frequently drives suppression more than the original emotion does.
Assertive expression practice Behavioural rehearsal of direct communication: stating a need or boundary clearly, at low intensity, without excessive qualification or escalation.

Questions people ask

Is this just anger management?

No. Anger is one part of it. Numbness, shutdown, emotional overwhelm, and difficulty expressing anything at all are the same underlying skill set, worked from the other direction.

I do not really feel things. Is that a problem?

Not necessarily, but if it is costing you closeness or leaving you unsure what you want, it is worth looking at. Numbness is usually protective, and it can be loosened safely.

Will you make me talk about feelings I am not ready for?

No. We work at the pace your nervous system can handle. Building the skills comes before going anywhere difficult.

How long before I notice a difference?

Most people leave the first couple of sessions with something practical to use. Changing a lifelong pattern takes longer, and we go at a realistic pace.

Can I bring my partner?

This page is individual work, but couples sessions are available if the pattern lives mostly in the relationship.

Does this help with emotional eating or drinking?

Often, yes. Those are frequently regulation strategies. We work on what they are managing rather than only on the behaviour.
